Test and validation engineering for physical hardware. The goal is to become competent at
turning product requirements into test plans, building and automating instrumented test
benches, running functional, performance, environmental and reliability tests on real
hardware, analysing the resulting data with sound statistics, and driving root cause
analysis and corrective action when a unit fails. This is the hands on discipline of
verification and validation for mechatronic, electromechanical, electronic and mechanical
products, distinct from pure design and from software quality assurance.

Measurement science and metrology. The foundation is the trustworthiness of every reading:
accuracy, precision and resolution, systematic and random error, the quantification of
measurement uncertainty and its propagation, calibration, and an unbroken traceability
chain back to national measurement standards. A measurement is only evidence if its
uncertainty is known.

Sensors, transducers and signal conditioning. Strain gauges and load cells read through a
Wheatstone bridge, thermocouples with cold junction compensation, resistance temperature
detectors, piezoelectric and IEPE accelerometers, rotary and linear encoders, linear
variable differential transformers, pressure and flow transducers. Each needs the correct
excitation, amplification, filtering and grounding and shielding to turn a physical quantity
into a clean electrical signal without corrupting it with noise.

Data acquisition and signal processing. Analog to digital conversion, sampling rate, the
Nyquist sampling theorem, aliasing and the anti aliasing filter, quantisation, oversampling,
windowing and the frequency content of a measured signal. The full chain from transducer
through conditioning to recorded data is treated as one path.

Engineering statistics and design of experiments. Probability distributions, descriptive
statistics, hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, regression and analysis of variance,
factorial and fractional factorial experiment design, response surface methods and Taguchi
robust design, so that multi factor tests are planned efficiently and conclusions are
defensible rather than anecdotal.

Statistical quality assurance and process control. Statistical process control and control
charts, process capability indices, acceptance sampling, measurement system analysis and
gauge repeatability and reproducibility, total quality and Six Sigma methods, quality
function deployment, so the measurement process itself and the production process are both
shown to be capable and in control.

Reliability engineering. Failure rate and hazard function, mean time between failures, the
bathtub curve, the Weibull and exponential life distributions, censored life data,
accelerated life testing and the Arrhenius model, reliability prediction and allocation,
maintainability and availability, design for reliability across the product life cycle, and
the reliability of electronic and mechanical assemblies.

Failure analysis and root cause. Failure mode and effects analysis, fault tree analysis,
practical root cause failure analysis, the five whys, fishbone diagrams, Pareto analysis and
the eight disciplines corrective action loop, closing the loop from an observed failure back
to a design or process fix.

Fatigue, durability, vibration and environmental testing. Fatigue of materials and
structures, stress life and strain life, damage accumulation and fatigue under random
loading, structural dynamics and modal testing, random vibration, shock, and durability and
environmental stress testing of products and structures. Model validation and uncertainty
quantification tie the physical test back to the engineering model.
