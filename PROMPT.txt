#   Thermal management theme controller using explicit model with proposed
#   controller design with data from test labs for verification
#
# Note: the tokenizer strips punctuation BEFORE splitting words, so hyphenated
# terms collapse into junk ("model-predictive" -> "modelpredictive"). Write

I am looking for work on thermal management and temperature control, where the
controller is built on an explicit process model rather than tuned purely by
hand or learned as a black box. The core interest is controller design for
thermal systems: regulating temperature, managing heat generation and heat
removal, and holding a thermal setpoint under changing load and changing
ambient conditions.

By explicit model I mean a model that is written down and used directly inside
the control law: a first principles physical model, an energy balance, a heat
transfer model, a lumped parameter or lumped capacitance thermal network, a
grey box or physics based model, a reduced order model, a state space
formulation with identified thermal resistance and thermal capacitance
parameters. Papers that derive the thermal dynamics analytically and then embed
those dynamics in the controller are the target. This includes explicit model
predictive control, model based predictive control, model based feedback
control, internal model control, feedforward compensation driven by the model,
and observer or state estimator designs such as a Kalman filter that
reconstruct unmeasured internal temperatures from surface or sensor
measurements.

I want papers that propose a controller design, not only papers that model or
simulate thermal behaviour. The proposed design should be described concretely:
the control structure, the control law and its derivation, tuning of the
controller parameters, stability analysis, constraint handling on temperature
limits and actuator effort, and the optimization or cost function if a
predictive or optimal control formulation is used. Comparison against a
baseline controller such as a proportional integral derivative controller, a
bang bang or on off thermostat, or a rule based strategy is welcome.

Verification must be grounded in measured data, not simulation alone. I am
looking for experimental validation using data from a test laboratory, a test
bench, a test rig, a test cell, a prototype, or a hardware in the loop setup:
model identification and calibration from laboratory measurements, model
validation against measured temperature trajectories, and closed loop
experiments that demonstrate tracking accuracy, overshoot, settling time,
steady state error, disturbance rejection, and energy consumption or efficiency
of the cooling strategy. Benchmarking the proposed controller against
laboratory test data, and quantifying the model error or prediction error
against those measurements, is central.

Relevant application domains include battery thermal management, electric
vehicle and power electronics cooling, electronics and chip cooling, heat
exchangers, heating ventilation and air conditioning, building thermal
management, furnaces and thermal processes, liquid cooling loops, fans, pumps,
coolant flow control, and thermoelectric or Peltier cooling.
