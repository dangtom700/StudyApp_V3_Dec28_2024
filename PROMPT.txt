Proton exchange membrane fuel cell modelling for closed-loop control. The goal is a
simplified but physically grounded dynamic model of a PEM fuel cell stack whose inputs are
genuine control variables, so that a feedback controller can be designed around it and the
optimal operating region identified.

The cell voltage model follows the polarization curve: the reversible Nernst open circuit
voltage minus activation overpotential, ohmic overpotential and concentration
overpotential, as a function of current density. Power density and net system power follow
from voltage times current density. The electrochemical model is parameterised by stack
temperature, oxygen partial pressure, hydrogen partial pressure, cathode and anode
pressure, membrane water content and membrane proton conductivity. Charge double layer
capacitance sets the fast transient voltage response. The exchange current density, the
limiting current density, the Tafel slope and the ohmic membrane resistance are fitted
parameters of the electrochemical model.

Controlled variables and manipulated inputs. The air supply subsystem is driven by a
compressor or blower whose motor voltage sets the cathode air mass flow rate through the
supply manifold and return manifold. The oxygen excess ratio, also called air
stoichiometry or oxygen stoichiometric ratio, is the primary controlled variable: too low
causes oxygen starvation and stack voltage collapse, too high wastes parasitic compressor
power and lowers net efficiency. There is therefore an optimal oxygen excess ratio setpoint
that maximises net system efficiency. Hydrogen supply is regulated by an anode inlet valve
or pressure regulator, with anode recirculation and purge. Cathode pressure is regulated by
a back pressure valve.

Thermal management and the temperature efficiency curve. A coolant loop with a pump and
radiator fan controls stack temperature. The lumped thermal model balances electrochemical
heat generation against coolant heat removal and ambient convection, giving the stack
temperature dynamics. Coolant mass flow rate and coolant inlet temperature are the
manipulated thermal inputs, and the stack operating temperature is the controlled variable.
Temperature affects reaction kinetics, membrane conductivity, water saturation pressure and
therefore efficiency.

Water and humidity management. Membrane hydration governs proton conductivity. Dehydration
raises ohmic resistance while flooding blocks the gas diffusion layer and the channels. The
relative humidity of the inlet air is set by a membrane humidifier or by injection, making
inlet relative humidity a manipulable input. Water transport combines electro osmotic drag
and back diffusion across the membrane. Liquid water saturation and two phase flow matter
for flooding.

Control design. State space formulation, linearization about an operating point, transfer
functions, system identification from input output data, observers and extended Kalman
filter state estimation for unmeasured states such as oxygen partial pressure and membrane
water content. Controllers of interest include PID and feedforward, gain scheduling,
feedback linearization, sliding mode control, robust control, adaptive control, linear
quadratic regulator, and model predictive control with constraint handling on voltage,
temperature and current limits. Extremum seeking and maximum efficiency point tracking
locate the optimum online. Multivariable coupling between the air loop, the thermal loop
and the humidity loop motivates multi input multi output control rather than single loop
control.

Analysis objectives. Simulate polarization curves of voltage versus current density and
power density versus current density across operating states. Map efficiency against
temperature, pressure, stoichiometry and humidity to find the optimal operating range.
Quantify the efficiency improvement of a closed loop feedback system compared with fixed
operating conditions. Study load following and transient response to a step change in
current draw, and the effect of disturbances and independent variables such as ambient
temperature, ambient pressure, altitude and inlet humidity. Implementation in MATLAB and
Simulink with a lumped parameter dynamic model, validated against experimental stack data.
